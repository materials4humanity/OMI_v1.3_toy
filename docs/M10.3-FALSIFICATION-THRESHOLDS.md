## 1. The procedure (Spec §9.4, ADR-041)

Spec §9.4 ties threshold-setting to §7.3's decision layer — already
implemented (`probability_of_conformance`, `cvar`, `asymmetric_cost`,
`src/omi/inverse.py`, ADR-033) — rather than to convention. Applied to a
falsification criterion whose observable residual is `R`:

1. **Name the decision `R` gates** — what continues, or is refused/
   augmented/redesigned, if the criterion's condition holds.
2. **Declare the two wrong-decision costs**: `cost_miss` (wrongly
   continuing to trust something that has actually failed the criterion)
   and `cost_false_alarm` (wrongly refusing/pausing when the criterion in
   fact holds) — Spec §7.3's `asymmetric_cost` sense. Declared,
   application-specific inputs; Core and Spec do not supply these, exactly
   as `𝒰_adm`'s numeric bound is a declared input rather than a framework
   one (E-27, `docs/V1.4-EDITS.md`).
3. **Declare the minimum effect worth catching**, `minimum_effect` — the
   smallest true departure from "the criterion holds" an application cares
   to detect. Also declared, not derived.
4. **Estimate the residual estimator's own sampling variability**,
   `standard_error`, empirically — repeated-campaign variation, or an
   oracle's known-truth calibration, at the declared campaign size. This
   is a *measured* fact about the estimator, not a declared input.
5. **Set the threshold** via `src/omi/inverse.py::decision_sensitive_threshold`
   (new, ADR-041): the Bayes-optimal boundary between `N(0,
   standard_error²)` ("the residual is noise") and `N(minimum_effect,
   standard_error²)` ("a real effect of at least `minimum_effect`"), under
   the declared asymmetric costs:

   ```
   τ = minimum_effect / 2  +  (standard_error² / minimum_effect) · ln(cost_false_alarm / cost_miss)
   ```

   The standard likelihood-ratio-test result for two equal-variance
   Gaussians, not an invented formula — verified against a numerical
   expected-cost sweep (`tests/test_inverse_decision_layer.py::test_decision_sensitive_threshold_minimises_expected_cost_against_a_numerical_sweep`).
6. **Report the threshold's sensitivity** to the declared cost ratio and
   `minimum_effect`. This — not a single number — is what makes the
   procedure "per application" (Spec §9.4's own phrase): §2 below shows a
   case where the ratio moves `τ` by more than 4× and a case where it is
   essentially inert, and the contrast between them is itself the
   reportable finding, not a nuisance to average away.

## 2. Worked value — Criterion 1 (sufficiency test persistently fails, bias-blocked)

**The decision.** Whether to accept a state description `𝒮` as sufficient
for a declared readout (here, flagship's `AggregateHardness`) and stop
augmenting it, versus continue investing in further state augmentation
(Spec §1.5's augmentation loop).

**Standard error, measured, not assumed.** 20 independent replications of
flagship's own real matched-pair sufficiency campaign
(`tests/test_conformance_flagship.py::_matched_pair_sufficiency_campaign`,
`n_pairs=2000` each):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Flagship `deficit_squared`: mean | `0.01086` |
| Flagship `deficit_squared`: standard error | `0.01609` |

As a calibration check on the deficit estimator itself (not flagship-
specific): 20 replications of the known-insufficiency oracle's own
campaign (`tests/oracles/known_insufficiency.py`, `n_pairs=8000`,
constructed truth `4.5`) recover `4.5086 ± 0.0540` — the estimator is
trustworthy at this precision, which is what licenses using its own
sampling variability as `standard_error` above rather than treating the
estimator itself as a source of unquantified doubt.

**Two illustrative declarations, computed with the same formula and the
same measured `standard_error = 0.01609`:**

| `minimum_effect` | `cost_false_alarm` | `cost_miss` | `τ` | Flagship mean (`0.01086`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1.0` | `1` | `20` | `0.49922` | not flagged |
| `1.0` | `20` | `1` | `0.50078` | not flagged |
| `0.05` | `1` | `20` | `0.00949` | **flagged** |
| `0.05` | `20` | `1` | `0.04051` | not flagged |

**Reading this, per step 6.** At `minimum_effect = 1.0` (a full hardness
unit of unexplained systematic gap — large relative to `standard_error`),
`τ` is essentially insensitive to the declared cost ratio: the two classes
("noise" vs. "a real 1.0-unit effect") are separated by roughly 60
standard errors, so no plausible cost asymmetry moves the boundary enough
to matter. At `minimum_effect = 0.05` — comparable to `standard_error`
itself, i.e. "catch even a small systematic gap" — the threshold becomes
genuinely decision-sensitive: it moves by more than 4× end to end as the
declared cost ratio flips, and flagship's own measured deficit sits
*between* the two resulting thresholds, meaning the same measured data
is flagged or not flagged depending on which application-specific cost
ratio is declared. Neither table row is tuned to produce this outcome; both
follow mechanically from the declared inputs and the measured
`standard_error` above.

**Contrast is not run for this criterion** (docs/DECISIONS.md ADR-041):
building an analogous matched-pair campaign for contrast is scoped out of
this milestone rather than rushed — this document reports flagship's real
number and the oracle's calibration, and states plainly that a contrast-
side matched-pair sufficiency campaign does not yet exist in this
repository, rather than filling the gap with a hastily-built one.

## 3. Worked value — Criterion 3's checkable symptom (super-linear rollout error growth)

**What is checkable, and what is not (E-16).** The raw measurement —
`omi.conformance.rollout_length_error_curve` — exists and runs on both
domains. "After all stabilisation measures" (Spec §2.5–2.7's regime table,
backward error budgeting, refusal criterion) is `[Pass B]` and
unimplemented (`amplification_decomposition` is an unconditional refusal
stub), so a positive finding here can never be read as *the* refutation
Core §6.1 names — only the symptom it would need to survive is reportable.

**The decision.** Whether to trust further chain composition without an
external correction step (assimilation, Core §3.9's error-control
dichotomy), versus treat growth as a signal that erasure/observation
density is inadequate for this chain length.

**Residual and standard error, measured.** "Excess over linear
extrapolation," `e_last − n_steps · e_first`, on both domains' current,
wholly-analytic chains, across 30 replications each (varying the incoming
ensemble draw and the metric it induces; the perturbed component and
magnitude are held fixed, matching the existing rollout-curve helpers in
`tests/test_conformance_flagship.py` and `tests/test_conformance_contrast.py`):

| Domain | Mean excess | Standard error | Chain length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flagship | `−0.033690` | `9.7 × 10⁻¹⁸` (effectively zero) | 2 steps |
| Contrast | `−35.000347` | `4.6 × 10⁻⁵` | 8 cycles |

Both are **deterministic and strongly negative** — sub-linear or flat, not
merely "not super-linear." The near-zero standard error is itself a
reportable fact about these specific analytic operators (both propagate
this perturbation homogeneously enough that repeated ensemble draws barely
perturb the ratio), not an artefact of too few replications.

**Applying the same formula.** With `standard_error` this close to zero,
`τ` collapses to `minimum_effect / 2` regardless of the declared cost
ratio (the correction term `standard_error² / minimum_effect · ln(...)`
vanishes):

| `minimum_effect` | `τ` | Flagship excess | Contrast excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0.01` | `0.00500` | far below | far below |
| `0.1` | `0.05000` | far below | far below |

**Reading this, per step 6 — the deliberate contrast with §2.** Here the
threshold is **decision-robust**, not decision-sensitive: no declared
`minimum_effect` or cost ratio an application would plausibly choose moves
the conclusion, because the measured excess is negative by roughly three
to four orders of magnitude more than any of the thresholds shown. Section
2's flagged/not-flagged split and this section's uniform "far below" are
both genuine, computed consequences of applying the identical procedure to
different measured data — the procedure does not manufacture sensitivity
where the data does not support it, nor flatten it where the data does.
