## 2 — The framework statistic

| quantity | 8 samples | 32 samples |
|---|---|---|
| separation, `INSUFFICIENT` vs `NOISY` | `0.9860` | `3.1626` |
| `z`, `INSUFFICIENT` | `1.7243` | `3.9957` |
| `z`, `NOISY` | `0.9925` | `1.4196` |
| `z`, `NULL` | — | `1.2242` |

**The two nuisance arms carry equal unexplained scatter by construction, and only the biased one
moves the statistic.** `z` on the `NOISY` arm sits at `1.4196`, between the null arm's `1.2242`
and nothing like the insufficiency arm's `3.9957` — which is what a statistic dividing a signed
mean by a *predicted* standard error should do when the extra scatter is zero-mean.

At 8 samples the separation is `0.9860`, i.e. **below one nuisance sigma**: the discrimination is
not available at the near length and only appears with accumulation. That is the axis working as
ADR-066 designed it and it is also a real limit on the claim — a campaign of eight samples cannot
make this call.

## 3 — The comparator's two separations, side by side

Required by the pre-registration's §5 so *blind* and *merely noisy* are distinguishable in the
output rather than in the interpretation.

| reading | separation | status |
|---|---|---|
| `INSUFFICIENT` vs **`NOISY`** (registered) | **`0.2324` σ** | criterion 3's quantity |
| `INSUFFICIENT` vs **`NULL`** (companion) | **`0.9937` σ** | not a criterion |
| ratio | **`4.28×`** | computed after the run — see below |

| arm | fitted noise / declared instrument sd |
|---|---|
| `NULL` | `0.9943` |
| `NOISY` | `1.1097` |
| `INSUFFICIENT` | `1.1748` |

**This is the attribution failure, in three numbers.** The comparator reads the declared
instrument precision correctly when nothing is planted (`0.9943`), and reads *both* nuisance arms
as carrying extra unexplained variance — `1.1097` and `1.1748`, within `6%` of each other. It
registers that something is unexplained and cannot say which of the two things it is. That is
exactly the mechanism ADR-065 stated in advance, and it is now measured rather than asserted.

### The label that fired, and why it is left as written

`SweepResult.comparator_reading` — written before the run — gates on whether the null-contrast
separation clears `1.0`, reusing criterion 3's ceiling for a second purpose. At the registered
replicate count that separation landed at `0.9937`, a hundredth below, so the label printed
**`AMBIGUOUS`**: "may be too noisy for either reading rather than unable to attribute".

**It is recorded verbatim and not rewritten.** But the gate is crude, and its being crude is
measurable: across replicate counts the two separations behave quite differently.

| replicates | registered contrast | null contrast |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | `0.4673` | `1.5744` |
| 16 | `0.4859` | `2.3256` |
| **24 (registered)** | **`0.2324`** | **`0.9937`** |

The registered-contrast separation never approaches `1.0`; the null-contrast separation straddles
it and is mostly well above. So the comparator's *pattern* is attribution-blindness — it separates
insufficiency from nothing several times better than from noise, at every count — and the
`AMBIGUOUS` label at 24 replicates is the crude gate flipping on a hundredth, which is the
"decided at machine precision" shape this repository filed as **E-48** in another context.

**The honest statement, therefore, has two parts and the second is a real limitation.**

1. The comparator cannot attribute: it reads the two nuisance arms as nearly the same.
2. **The strength of that "cannot" is limited by the comparator's own variance.** Its
   null-contrast separation swings `0.99`–`2.33` across replicate counts. A reader should take
   the claim as established on the *ratio* and the per-arm means, not on criterion 3's margin
   alone — because a ceiling of `1.0` is easy to sit below when your instrument is noisy.

## 4 — The calibration, checked on the judged seeds

The arms were matched on innovation variance at seeds `2000..2023`. The sweep judges seeds
`0..23`, so the match there is an **outcome**:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| `var(d)`, `INSUFFICIENT` | `0.0050354` |
| `var(d)`, `NOISY` | `0.0047650` |
| relative mismatch | **`5.37%`** (calibrated to `0.05%` on the calibration seeds) |

**The direction favours the claim and is stated for that reason.** The insufficiency arm carries
`5.4%` more innovation variance than the noise arm on the judged campaigns — about `2.7%` in
standard deviation — so the noise arm is a marginally *weaker* nuisance than intended. The
mismatch was **not re-tuned**: the inflation factor is a registered value and adjusting it after
seeing the sweep is exactly what pre-registration forbids. Reported, quantified, left alone.

## 6 — What this does and does not establish

**Establishes, on one construction, one domain and one acquisition:** a campaign monitor built
from the model's own *predicted* innovation covariance separates a state insufficiency from
matched exploration noise (`3.16` σ, growing `3.21×` along the axis), where a GP acquisition's
fitted noise term does not (`0.23` σ) because it absorbs both into the same parameter.

**Does not establish, and the pre-registration said so in advance:**

- anything about **symmetric** insufficiencies — a signed monitor is blind to those, and this
  construction's latent is one-sided by design;
- anything about insufficiencies that contribute mostly **variance** rather than a mean shift.
  The calibrated inflation came out small (`1.160`) precisely because this latent's variance
  contribution is modest, which is the same fact from the other side;
- anything at **short campaign length** — the separation is below one sigma at 8 samples;
- that the framework *predicts* better. It does not: the GP searches at least as well in every
  arm, and ADR-065 said it would.

**And one thing this sweep's own numbers weaken.** The comparator's discrimination is weak in
absolute terms on *both* contrasts and unstable across replicate counts. The claim's comparative
half rests on the comparator reading the two arms as nearly identical, which it does; it does not
rest on criterion 3's threshold, which a noisy instrument clears too easily. §3's ratio and
per-arm means are the citable evidence.
