# M11.5 — the fair-axis extrapolation experiment, Generator C

**Result: the claim is REFUTED. Declared constitutive forms buy no extrapolation
reach on this axis — every declared-form arm is worse than the free-form operator,
and an unchanged tabular baseline beats all of them.**

**And unlike M11.4, this null means something.** The hold-out was gated before
registration: the axis carries signal (3.806 against M11.4's exactly 0.000) and the
two model classes diverge along it (14.57 against M11.4's 1.05). The comparison
could have detected an effect. There was none.

Hold-out gated and thresholds fixed at `97621ac`, before this sweep script existed
(`docs/M11.5-PREREGISTRATION.md`, whose §5 discloses what had already been seen).
Design: ADR-047 under ADR-045 as amended.
Reproduce: `python scripts/run_m11_5_extrapolation.py`.

---

## 1. The pre-registered result

Held-out accumulated strains `{2, 3, 4, 6}` against a declared window ending at
`γ = 1.0` — extrapolation factor **min 2.00, median 3.50, max 6.00**, so every
evaluation point is outside the declared range. Criterion: held-out RMSE **pooled**
over all (query, strain) pairs, as registered.

| Contestant | held-out RMSE | sd | declared form? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 GBT | **3.26607** | 0.042 | no |
| 1 free-form rate law | **5.06181** | 0.097 | no |
| 3b missing dependence | 7.56449 | 0.733 | yes |
| 2 correct form | 8.10280 | 0.114 | yes |
| 4 Ridge | 24.31240 | 0.382 | no |
| 3a missing mechanism | 69.54710 | 1.470 | yes |

| Comparison | gap | τ (pre-registered) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 − 3a** | −64.485 | 0.331 | **WORSE** |
| **1 − 3b** | −2.503 | 0.322 | **WORSE** |
| 1 − 2 (ceiling) | −3.041 | 0.315 | WORSE |
| 1 − 4 Ridge | −19.251 | 0.316 | WORSE |
| 1 − 4 GBT | +1.796 | 0.317 | EFFECT **for the baseline** |

**`gap(3, 1)` and `gap(3, 4)` — the claim — are not merely unsupported, they are
negative by margins one to two hundred times the registered threshold.** Declaring
a constitutive form and getting it canonically right but locally wrong is, on this
axis, *worse* than declaring nothing. The only comparison that clears its threshold
clears it in the tabular baseline's favour.

**Contestant 2 carries ADR-045's caveat and is reported as a ceiling.** Because the
training region is noiseless and the generator is exactly this form there, it
recovers `k₁ = 8.000`, `k₂₀ = 2.000`, `p = 1.500` to machine precision — so its
held-out error is the irreducible cost of not knowing the withheld term, with no
parameter-estimation error mixed in. The ceiling is **8.10**, and two contestants
without any declared form beat it.

**3a and 3b diverge enormously: +61.98 against a declared minimum effect of 0.63.**
The question left open at M11.4 — whether the effect depends on *which kind* of
misspecification the declared physics carries — is answered here, and answered in
the direction of harm. See §3.

## 2. Why the declared forms lose, measured rather than argued

The declared forms are not losing because they are wrong about their own physics.
Scored against a ground truth with the withheld recrystallisation term **removed** —
"how well does each contestant capture everything it could in principle know?" — the
ranking nearly inverts:

| Contestant | vs full truth (registered) | **vs withheld-free truth** | rank change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 correct form | 8.10 (4th) | **0.0000** (1st) | +3 |
| 1 free-form | 5.06 (2nd) | 3.7752 (2nd) | — |
| 3b missing dependence | 7.56 (3rd) | 5.4362 (3rd) | — |
| 4 GBT | **3.27 (1st)** | 8.5214 (4th) | **−3** |
| 4 Ridge | 24.31 (5th) | 17.0895 (5th) | — |
| 3a missing mechanism | 69.55 (6th) | 63.1420 (6th) | — |

**The correct declared form is exactly right about the physics it declares and
finishes fourth. The winner is fourth on that same measure.**

The mechanism is error cancellation, and it is measurable as a signed bias rather
than inferred. The withheld term removes density: its mean signed effect on the
truth is **−6.564**.

| Contestant | bias vs withheld-free truth | bias vs full truth | cancels? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 GBT | **−7.487** | **−0.923** | yes, almost exactly |
| 1 free-form | −2.702 | +3.861 | yes, ~41% |
| 3b missing dependence | −1.553 | +5.010 | yes, ~24% |
| 2 correct form | **+0.000** | **+6.564** | no — carries the full cost |
| 4 Ridge | +13.342 | +19.906 | no, compounds |
| 3a missing mechanism | +48.810 | +55.374 | no, compounds |

**The gradient-boosted baseline wins by being wrong in the right direction.** It
under-predicts the physics it should capture by −7.49, the withheld term reduces the
truth by −6.56, and the two nearly annihilate, leaving a full-truth bias of −0.92.
The correct declared form's bias against the physics it expresses is *exactly zero*,
so it pays the withheld term's cost in full.

**This does not un-refute the claim, and must not be read as doing so.** The
registered criterion was held-out RMSE, the registered claim was that declared forms
would beat free-form and tabular contestants on it, and they did not. What the
diagnostic establishes is a *second, separate* finding about the criterion — see §4.

## 3. Which kind of misspecification, answered

M11.4 could not separate the two arms (+0.315 against a minimum effect of 0.702).
Here they differ by **+61.98**, and the practical reading is unambiguous:

- **A missing *dependence* costs almost nothing.** 3b treats a temperature-dependent
  recovery coefficient as constant and scores 7.56 against the correct form's 8.10 —
  it is *better* than getting the form fully right, because its fitted constant
  `k₂ = 1.701` sits below the true average and the resulting under-prediction
  cancels a quarter of the withheld term. Declaring a form you are only partly sure
  of, where the doubt is about a parameter's functional dependence, is close to free.
- **A missing *mechanism* is catastrophic.** 3a drops the recovery term entirely and
  scores **69.55**, thirteen times the free-form baseline and twenty-one times the
  tabular one. With nothing to balance storage it cannot saturate, so it diverges
  with accumulated strain: per-strain error runs 11.5 → 30.4 → 56.2 → **123.0**.

**Declaring a form with a mechanism missing is far worse than declaring no form at
all.** That is the single most actionable result of the M11 track, and it is a
warning rather than an endorsement: the extension's value proposition is asymmetric,
with a small upside for parameter-level doubt and a very large downside for
structural doubt — and a practitioner cannot always tell which kind they have.

## 4. Per-strain curves, and what they add

Reported per CLAUDE.md invariant 7. **Not** the registered criterion: the registered
figure is the pooled one in §1, and reading a gap off whichever strain flatters it is
exactly what the pre-registration forbids.

| Contestant | γ=2 | 3 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 free-form | 0.71 | 2.35 | 4.68 | 8.63 |
| 2 correct form | 1.43 | 4.75 | 8.22 | 13.05 |
| 3a missing mechanism | 11.52 | 30.45 | 56.19 | 123.00 |
| 3b missing dependence | 3.41 | 5.25 | 7.60 | 11.48 |
| 4 Ridge | 4.76 | 12.74 | 22.26 | 41.03 |
| 4 GBT | 3.97 | 3.45 | 1.80 | 3.43 |

The ordering is stable across the whole held-out range — no contestant's verdict
depends on which strain is read — so the pooled criterion is not hiding a crossover.
Two shapes are worth naming. **3a's error grows without bound**, which is the
signature of a model that cannot saturate. **GBT's error is flat and non-monotone**,
which is the signature of a model that is not extrapolating at all: past the training
range its prediction is essentially constant, and it happens to be constant near
where the recrystallised truth sits.

**M11.4's untested hypothesis is answered, and it does not survive.** M11.4 recorded
a γ=4 separation — declared-form arms at 18–20 against free-form and tabular at
40–41 — as a pre-specified observation generating an untested hypothesis, and
flagged that its contestant 1 extrapolated as `surface × γ`. Tested here against a
free-form contestant that is an *operator* rather than a surface, the separation
**reverses**: free-form 4.68 against the correct form's 8.22 at γ=4. The M11.4
observation was an artefact of that baseline's extrapolation rule, as ADR-047
suspected it might be. Recording it as untested was correct; it is now tested and
refuted.

## 5. What is and is not claimed

**Claimed.** On Generator C, with a hold-out gated for discrimination before
registration, thresholds pre-registered in a prior commit, and a free-form baseline
that is an integrated operator rather than a response surface, **declared
constitutive forms show no extrapolation benefit and a substantial penalty.** The
best contestant carries no declared form. A missing mechanism is catastrophically
worse than no declared form; a missing dependence is not.

**Claimed, separately.** The declared forms' failure is not ignorance of their own
physics. The correct form is exact about everything it expresses; it loses because
its competitors' extrapolation errors partially cancel the withheld term and its own
do not.

**Not claimed.** That declared physics buys no reach *in general*. One generator, one
axis, one domain, no observation noise. In particular, noiselessness removes the
mechanism by which declared forms are conventionally argued to help — parsimony under
limited data — and that is the obvious next experiment.

**Not claimed.** That the tabular baseline is a better model. It is fourth of six at
the physics it could have learned and wins on a near-exact cancellation. Under a
different withheld term it would lose by the same margin it won by.

## 6. Recorded as a defect in the framework, not only in this build

Spec §9.3 prescribes a baseline comparison on forward accuracy and says nothing
about decomposing that error into "the physics the model could express" and "the
physics that was withheld". This experiment shows the two come apart hard enough to
**invert the ranking**: the model that is exactly right about the expressible physics
finishes fourth, and the model that wins is fourth on that measure. Filed as
`docs/V1.4-EDITS.md` **E-42**.

The sting is that the diagnostic which reveals it needs a generator you can modify,
so it is available in a synthetic study and **not** on field data — where a win by
error cancellation and a win by knowing the physics are indistinguishable. That is
the same unavailability E-39 noted for nulls, now measured for positive results.

## 7. Standing limitations, carried forward from the pre-registration

- **Standard errors were measured in-envelope**, which may understate held-out
  variability and makes τ tighter. Since every declared-form verdict is WORSE by
  margins one to two hundred times τ, no verdict here is sensitive to it.
- **The gate is a dry run at shorter reach** (`γ = 1.0`) than the sweep (`γ = 6.0`).
- **A single generator.** ADR-045's Generator B was not run and no cross-generator
  comparison was computed.
- **§5 of the pre-registration discloses a peek** at held-out numbers before the
  thresholds were fixed. The peek's reading and this result agree in direction, and
  both are negative for the extension this repository is arguing for.
