# v1.5 Part 6 pre-registration — the campaign experiment

**Committed before the sweep exists.** ADR-045's requirement, applied: this document and
`tests/oracles/part6_thresholds.py` are committed **on their own**, so the ordering is visible
in `git log` rather than asserted in a report. No sweep code, no `NOISY`-arm construction and
no comparison of the registered contrast exists at this commit.

Design: ADR-063 (the statistic), ADR-064 (the operators), ADR-065 (the comparator and the third
arm), ADR-066 (the planted insufficiency and the axis). Gate measurements and the two framework
findings that came out of building it: `docs/V1.5-PART6.md`.

**§6 discloses, in full, what was already seen before these thresholds were fixed.** Read it
before reading anything here as a guarantee.

---

## 1. The claim

> The framework identifies when a campaign is failing due to model insufficiency rather than
> exploration noise; a GP-based acquisition cannot.

**Narrow, and deliberately.** It is not that the framework predicts better — ADR-065 states in
advance that the GP handles in-distribution uncertainty better and searches better. It is that
one specific attribution is available to a model with a *predicted* innovation covariance and
not to one that *fits* its own noise level.

## 2. The design, in one table

| | |
|---|---|
| domain | discovery (`src/omi_domains/sdl/`), the only domain with an acquisition-determined control axis (ADR-060) |
| campaign | GP-Expected-Improvement over the declared attainable region; the GP drives **all** arms |
| axis | campaign length in samples, **8 → 32**, near and far being prefixes of one campaign |
| arms | `NULL` (nothing planted) · `INSUFFICIENT` (hidden `precursor_texture`) · `NOISY` (inflated observation noise, calibrated) |
| framework statistic | `z_n = \|mean(d)\| / √(mean(S)/n)` over the campaign's pooled forecast innovations (ADR-063) |
| comparator statistic | the GP's fitted noise level over the declared instrument precision (ADR-065) |
| **registered contrast** | **`INSUFFICIENT` versus `NOISY`** |
| replicates | 24 campaigns per arm |

**Why three arms.** The claim's contrast is *insufficiency versus exploration noise*. With only a
null and an insufficiency arm the GP's fitted noise separates the arms perfectly well, so a
two-arm design measures detection rather than attribution and cannot evaluate the claim at all
(ADR-065). `NOISY` is an arm that **is** exploration noise, sized so the comparator cannot tell
it from insufficiency.

## 3. Step 1 — the precondition, already passed and already reported

E-41's criteria, re-verified on the discovery domain rather than inherited from Part 5(1)'s
contrast dry-run (`docs/V1.5-PART6.md` §3):

| criterion | quantity | measured |
|---|---|---|
| the axis carries signal | planted effect in accumulated noise-widths | `1.75` → `4.61` |
| the statistic diverges | `growth_ratio` | **`2.909`** against a declared bar of `2.0` |
| the candidate has content | separation against the comparator, **on the null contrast** | `3.948` vs `0.994` |

Verdict on the `INSUFFICIENT`-versus-`NULL` contrast: **`DISCRIMINATING`**. Criterion 3 on the
**registered** contrast is deferred to the sweep, because there the baseline comparison *is* the
registered quantity — the collision is filed as **E-58** and not resolved by relaxing anything.

## 4. Step 2 — the pre-registered thresholds

### 4.1 The monitor's control limit

| input | value | basis |
|---|---|---|
| `NULL_Z_MEAN` | `0.926` | measured over 40 null campaigns |
| `NULL_Z_Q95` | `2.213` | measured 95th percentile of the same |
| `CONTROL_LIMIT` | **`2.213`** | the **measured** null quantile, not the normal `1.960` |

**Why the measured quantile and not the declared normal one.** ADR-063 declares a standard-normal
reference, and the innovation spread matches the filter's own prediction to `0.2%`, so the
statistic *is* standardised. But innovations within one campaign are mildly correlated, putting
the null `z` about 16% above `E|Z| = 0.798`. Using `1.960` would be anti-conservative **in the
direction of the claim**. The measured limit is used and the discrepancy is disclosed rather than
absorbed.

### 4.2 The registered decision rule

**Criterion:** the separation of the `INSUFFICIENT` arm from the `NOISY` arm, in `NOISY`-arm sigma
units, at the **far** campaign length (32 samples), computed for both instruments.

| threshold | value | basis |
|---|---|---|
| `REQUIRED_SEPARATION` | **`2.0`** | two nuisance-arm sigmas — the same "clearly above scatter" bar Part 5(1)'s dry-run used, so the two gates read on one scale |
| `REQUIRED_GROWTH` | **`2.0`** | carried over unchanged from `tests/oracles/test_statistic_dry_run.py`; the statistic's own closed form predicts `√(32/8) = 2.000` under a constant bias, so this is the floor the arithmetic already guarantees and not a bar chosen to be clearable |
| `COMPARATOR_CEILING` | **`1.0`** | the comparator "cannot attribute" if its own separation on the registered contrast is **below one** nuisance-arm sigma |

**The claim is supported if and only if all three hold:**

1. `separation(innovation_mean_z; I vs X) ≥ 2.0` at 32 samples;
2. `growth_ratio(innovation_mean_z; I vs X) ≥ 2.0` between 8 and 32 samples;
3. `separation(gp_noise_excess_ratio; I vs X) < 1.0` at 32 samples.

Per-length curves are reported alongside (CLAUDE.md invariant 7) but are **not** the registered
criterion — picking the campaign length at which the gap looks best is what this rule forbids.

### 4.3 The third arm's calibration, fixed now

The arms are matched on a **physical** quantity: the variance of the declared model's forecast
innovations — the unexplained scatter a practitioner actually sees. Innovation variance is affine
in the squared inflation, so two grid points solve it exactly and no search is involved.

| input | value | basis |
|---|---|---|
| `var(d)`, `INSUFFICIENT` | `0.0047509` | measured, 24 seeds disjoint from the sweep's |
| `var(d)`, `NOISY` at inflation `1.0` / `1.4` | `0.0035326` / `0.0069235` | the two solving grid points |
| **`NOISE_INFLATION`** | **`1.160`** | solved, then held fixed |
| `var(d)`, `NOISY` at `1.160` | `0.0047533` | a match to **`0.05%`** |

So the two arms have **equal unexplained scatter, and only one has a biased mean.** That is the
decomposition the whole claim turns on, and it is a property of the construction rather than a
result.

**This replaced a circular first design, and the correction is the reason this subsection is
worth reading.** ADR-066's first version matched the arms on the *comparator's* fitted noise.
That sets the numerator of the comparator's separation to approximately zero — so criterion 3
would have been **true by construction**, and the experiment would have had one real criterion
while reporting three. Matching the innovation variance instead fixes neither instrument's
reading: the framework statistic is a function of the innovation *mean*, and the comparator's is
a fitted noise level on a surface over composition, not a moment of the declared model's
residual. Both readings on the registered contrast remain outcomes.

**A consequence that is favourable to the claim and therefore stated here rather than left to be
noticed.** The solved inflation is small (`1.160`) because the latent contributes modestly to
innovation *variance* and substantially to the innovation *mean*. So arm X will look much like
arm N to any variance-based diagnostic. That is the honest content of the claim — a bias and a
scatter are different things, and only an instrument with a *predicted* covariance separates
them — but it also means this experiment tests a construction whose discriminable signal is a
mean shift. **An insufficiency contributing mostly variance would not be detected by this
statistic at all**, and the sweep must not be read as evidence about that case.

## 5. What a null result means — pre-committed as publishable

**A null is a real possibility and is not a failure to be worked around.** Three specific nulls:

1. **Both instruments separate I from X** (criterion 3 fails). The claim is **refuted**: a fitted
   noise term is enough for this attribution and the operator graph is not needed for it. This is
   the most useful of the three to an SDL practitioner and will be reported as the headline.
2. **Neither separates them** (criterion 1 fails). The framework's diagnostic is no better than
   the comparator's on the one contrast it was selected for. Published as a **bound on what a
   signed innovation monitor buys** — and a real one, because the statistic was selected by a
   dry-run on a *different* domain, so a failure here is M11.4's non-transfer finding in a new
   place.
3. **The comparator is too noisy for either reading.** On the null contrast the GP's separation is
   `0.994` — its weakness there is variance, not blindness. If it also cannot separate I from X,
   "cannot attribute" may really be "too noisy to do either". The sweep therefore reports the
   comparator's **I-vs-N separation alongside its I-vs-X separation**, and if the two are
   comparable the claim is recorded as **not evaluated** rather than as supported.

**A null does not license re-selecting the statistic.** Part 5(1) chose `innovation_bias` from
three candidates on contrast. Re-running that dry-run on discovery to find a candidate that works
would be selecting on the registered quantity, and it is forbidden here in advance.

## 6. Disclosure: what was already seen before these thresholds were fixed

Everything in `docs/V1.5-PART6.md` was measured before this document existed, and it is the gate
the brief required. Precisely:

**Seen.** The framework statistic and the comparator statistic on the `INSUFFICIENT` and `NULL`
arms at both campaign lengths, with separations, growth ratios and per-arm means; the axis
signal; the null-arm calibration of the monitor over 40 seeds; the erasure measurement; the
campaign's search behaviour.

**Not seen, stated precisely rather than loosely.** `Arm.NOISY` is declared in the enum at the
previous commit and `run_campaign` accepts a `noise_inflation` argument, so the arm is
*constructible* there — but nothing constructs it, and neither instrument has ever been evaluated
on it. At **this** commit the arm is constructed for the calibration, which reads only the
**variance** of the declared model's innovations. **The registered quantity — the
`INSUFFICIENT`-versus-`NOISY` separation, for either instrument — is unmeasured at both commits.**

**Three things changed in response to what was seen, and each is a correction rather than a
tuning.** All three are recorded in the code they changed:

1. **An off-by-one in the harness** read the truth one evaluation interval before the forecast,
   producing a spurious null-arm bias larger than the planted effect and in the same direction.
   Fixed.
2. **The observation schedule** moved from every interval to one endpoint per sample, because
   within-sample assimilation absorbed the bias. That is Core §3.9's condition (b) working, and it
   became half of **E-57**; the statistic now reads the cross-sample forecast innovation, which is
   what a campaign actually cannot predict.
3. **The latent's distribution** moved from zero-mean to half-normal, because a zero-mean
   multiplicative latent gives a bias that is *second order* in its spread and left the experiment
   underpowered. The physical justification is prior to the measurement — a disorder density has
   a floor at zero — but the measurement is what prompted looking.

**And two changes that cut against the claim, both found while drafting this document.** The
first is the calibration rule: matching the arms on the comparator's own statistic would have made
criterion 3 true by construction, and it was replaced with a physical match on innovation
variance (§4.3, ADR-066 as amended). The second: the comparator's diagnostic was first normalised by
the campaign's own target spread, which made the GP read *lower* under insufficiency than under
the null — a confound favouring the framework. It was replaced with normalisation by the declared
instrument precision. **A confound that flatters the claim is exactly the kind that has to be
found before the thresholds, and it was.**

**The direction of the disclosure, stated plainly.** The numbers seen are favourable to the
framework on the null contrast. That is a genuine weakness of this pre-registration relative to
one written before any measurement — and it is unavoidable here, because the gate the brief
required *is* a measurement on those arms. What limits the damage is that criterion 3, the only
criterion the claim actually turns on, is a quantity on a contrast that does not yet exist.

## 7. Standing limitations, in advance

- **The monitor is blind to symmetric insufficiencies.** A signed statistic detects a non-zero
  mean effect. This construction plants a one-sided one, which is a real class and not the only
  class (ADR-066).
- **One planted insufficiency, one domain, one acquisition.** No cross-domain or cross-policy
  comparison will be computed.
- **The `NOISY` arm's calibration is at the near length only** and may drift at the far length.
  Reported as an outcome.
- **The discovery domain declares an erasure**, so E-54's narrow form applies: its
  observed/inferred readings sit at the trivial extreme, and **nothing in this experiment rests on
  them** (`docs/V1.5-E53-FIX.md` §5).
- **The GP is a GP over composition alone** — the acquisition the claim names, not a hybrid given
  the declared state (ADR-065 rejects that as unfair in the other direction).
- **The sweep is a separate authorisation.** It is not part of v1.5 planning's gate.
