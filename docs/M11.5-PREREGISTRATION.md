# M11.5 pre-registration — the fair-axis experiment

**Committed before the sweep exists.** ADR-045 requires the thresholds to precede
the run; this document, `scripts/run_m11_5_preregistration.py`,
`src/omi/proposed/holdout.py`, `src/omi_domains/flagship_constitutive/strain_experiment.py`
and `tests/test_holdout_discrimination.py` are committed on their own so the
ordering is visible in `git log` rather than asserted in a report. No sweep code,
no held-out evaluation and no contestant comparison exists at this commit.

Design: ADR-047, under ADR-045 as amended. Threshold procedure: ADR-041's
`omi.inverse.decision_sensitive_threshold`.

**§5 discloses, in full, what was already seen before these thresholds were fixed.**
Read it before reading anything else here as a guarantee.

---

## 1. What is being tested, and why again

M11.4 asked whether constraining an operator to a declared constitutive form buys
extrapolation reach, held out the strain-*rate* axis, and produced a null that was
**uninformative rather than negative**: Kocks–Mecking has no strain-rate
dependence, so the declared form and the free-form baseline agreed by construction
along exactly the axis withheld (`docs/M11.4-EXTRAPOLATION.md`;
`docs/V1.4-EDITS.md` E-39).

M11.5 is the same experiment on an axis where the declared form has content.
Everything in ADR-045 — four contestants, two misspecification arms, cost ratio,
threshold procedure, the ceiling caveat on contestant 2, the negative-result rule —
carries over. Three things change with the axis, and one changes because it did.

## 2. The design

**The held-out axis is accumulated strain.** Kocks–Mecking's content is a balance
between storage and recovery that produces saturation, and saturation is a claim
about accumulated strain. Training draws `γ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}`; evaluation
draws `γ ∈ {2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 6.0}`, outside `KOCKS_MECKING_STRAIN_WINDOWED`'s declared
window. Strain rate and temperature are drawn from the **same** ranges on both
sides, so accumulated strain is the only quantity withheld and every gap stays
attributable. Grouped by region, never randomly (CLAUDE.md invariant 6).

**Generator C withholds one named term the declared form can see.**

```
dρ/dγ = k₁√ρ − k₂(T)·ρ − k_drx·ρ·max(0, γ − γ_c)
```

Dynamic recrystallisation consuming stored density above a critical strain.
Identically zero below `γ_c`, and `γ_c` is set at the upper edge of the training
range — so over the whole training set contestant 2's form is *exactly* the
generator's, and "outside the declared window" and "the withheld physics is active"
name the same region by construction. The term is proportional to `ρ`, the same
shape as Kocks–Mecking's own recovery term, so a fitted declared form can **partly
absorb** it by inflating `k₂`. That is what makes the two misspecification arms
interpretable rather than jointly doomed.

**Contestant 1 is a free-form rate law, integrated — and this is the crux.**
M11.4's contestant 1 extrapolated as `surface(q) × γ`, linear in accumulated strain
by construction. On a strain hold-out that is a straw man, not a baseline: it cannot
saturate, so the declared form would win for a reason unrelated to declared physics.
Contestant 1 here is a generic quadratic rate law in `(ρ, ln γ̇, T/T_ref)` — ten
terms, the same dimensionality M11.4's carried — advanced by the **same integrator
at the same fixed step size** as every declared-form contestant, with non-negativity
enforced architecturally in the integrator rather than as a fitting penalty. An
integrated rate law that learns `f(ρ) < 0` at large `ρ` **can** saturate. It is not
given a `√ρ` term, which is the declared form's own content.

**Every fitted contestant uses the same optimiser** (`scipy.optimize.least_squares`,
identical tolerances and evaluation budget), so none is advantaged by fit quality.
This matters more here than at M11.4 because contestant 1 now carries ten parameters
against the declared forms' one to three.

| | Contestant | Declared form? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free-form **rate law**, integrated; positivity architectural | no |
| 2 | Correct form, parameters fitted — **ceiling arm, not the claim** | yes |
| 3a | Missing *mechanism*: recovery term dropped | yes |
| 3b | Missing *dependence*: recovery coefficient treated as constant | yes |
| 4 | Tabular baselines from `omi.baseline`, unchanged, with `γ` as a feature | no |

**A property of a noiseless design, stated because it looks like a flaw and is
not.** Below `γ_c` the generator *is* contestant 2's form exactly, and the training
labels carry no observation noise, so contestant 2 recovers `k₁ = 8.000`,
`k₂₀ = 2.000`, `p = 1.500` to machine precision. This is **not** E-12's circularity:
the ground truth over the held-out region is not contestant 2's form, and its
held-out error is not zero. What exact recovery buys is a *cleaner ceiling* than
M11.4's — contestant 2's held-out error is the irreducible cost of not knowing the
withheld term, with no parameter-estimation error mixed in.

Noiselessness is also the **conservative** choice for the claim under test. Noise
would introduce a second mechanism by which declared physics helps — parsimony
under limited data — and confound attribution. A noiseless design removes that
advantage and leaves only extrapolation reach, which is what is being measured. A
noise study is a separate experiment and is not claimed here.

## 3. Step 1 — the hold-out-discrimination gate

ADR-045's amendment makes M11.4's post-mortem a precondition:
`omi.proposed.holdout.check_hold_out_discriminates`. It runs **strictly
in-envelope** — fit on `γ ∈ {0.25, 0.5}`, probe at `γ = 0.5` (the fitted region's
upper edge) and `γ = 1.0` (the envelope's) — and never touches the held-out
strains, so it leaks nothing about the quantity the thresholds will judge.

| | M11.5, accumulated strain | M11.4, strain rate (for comparison) |
|---|---|---|
| withheld-free truth's own signal along the axis | 3.80564 | **0.00000** |
| disagreement at the fitted edge | 0.03240 | 0.33865 |
| disagreement at the envelope edge | 0.47193 | 0.35388 |
| **divergence** | **14.567** | **1.045** |
| candidate error on withheld-free truth | 0.00000 | 0.31600 |
| baseline error on withheld-free truth | 0.47193 | 0.06920 |
| noise floor | 0.02836 | 0.30712 |
| **verdict** | **DISCRIMINATING** | **INERT_AXIS** |

`required_divergence = 2.0`, declared before either column was read.

**M11.4's axis fails all three criteria independently** — the truth is exactly
constant along it, the models are parallel, and the declared form is the *worse* of
the two at the physics it does express. That is the retro-validation that this gate
discriminates rather than decorates, and it is pinned by
`tests/test_holdout_discrimination.py`.

**Building the gate corrected E-39's own proposed wording, which is the more
valuable half of it.** E-39 proposed requiring "materially different predictions"
across the held-out region. The two axes' far-point disagreements are 0.472 and
0.354 — the same order — so **no threshold on that quantity admits the good axis
without also admitting the bad one.** The wording is inoperable, not merely
imprecise. What separates them is whether the truth varies along the axis at all,
and whether the models *diverge* as the axis is pushed. Filed as
`docs/V1.4-EDITS.md` **E-41**.

## 4. Step 2 — the pre-registered thresholds

Same procedure, same declared costs as M11.4, so the two milestones' verdicts are
read on the same scale.

| Input | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `required_divergence` | **2.0** | the gate above; declared before it was run |
| `minimum_effect` | **0.629566** | 10% of the withheld term's mean absolute contribution over the held-out region (6.29566) |
| `cost_false_alarm` | 3.0 | a false alarm puts an unsupported claim in a paper; three times a miss |
| `cost_miss` | 1.0 | |
| `standard_error` | per comparison, below | seed-to-seed sd of the gap estimator, measured **in-envelope only** |

The withheld term's magnitude is a property of the *generator*, computed by running
it twice and differencing. No contestant is fitted, no prediction made, no error
measured, so consulting it is design information rather than a look at the result.

| Comparison | gap sd (in-envelope) | **τ** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 − 2 (correct form) | 0.00557 | **0.31484** |
| 1 − 3a (missing mechanism) | 0.09608 | **0.33089** |
| 1 − 3b (missing dependence) | 0.06348 | **0.32181** |
| 1 − 4 Ridge | 0.02855 | **0.31621** |
| 1 − 4 GBT | 0.03857 | **0.31738** |

**Decision rule, fixed now.** The criterion is **held-out RMSE pooled over all
(query, strain) pairs at `γ ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6}`**. A gap counts as a real effect only if
it exceeds that comparison's τ. Per-strain curves are reported alongside (CLAUDE.md
invariant 7) but are **not** the registered criterion — picking the strain at which
a gap looks best is precisely what this rule forbids, and it is the error M11.4's
report flagged in its own γ=4 observation.

## 5. Disclosure: what was already seen before these thresholds were fixed

**This pre-registration is weaker than M11.4's, and the reason is recorded here
rather than left for a reader to discover.** While building the machinery I ran a
full held-out evaluation of all six contestants in a scratch script, before the
thresholds in §4 existed. That is a peek at the pre-registered quantity. What was
seen, quoted so nothing is hidden:

| Contestant | in-envelope RMSE | held-out RMSE | vs withheld-free truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 free-form | 0.0496 | 4.9652 | 3.7752 |
| 2 correct form | 0.0000 | 8.2037 | 0.0000 |
| 3a missing mechanism | 1.7312 | 70.5726 | 63.1420 |
| 3b missing dependence | 1.0793 | 6.8288 | 5.4362 |
| 4 Ridge | 0.6050 | 24.3185 | 17.0895 |
| 4 GBT | 0.1759 | 3.3052 | 8.5214 |

Four things about this, all of which matter to how much the pre-registration is
still worth.

1. **No design parameter was changed in response to those numbers.** The generator,
   its withheld term's strength, the training and held-out strain sets, the control
   ranges, the contestants and their bases, and the campaign size are all as they
   were when that run was made.
2. **Three things did change afterwards, none of them a response to a contestant's
   score.** The dry-run probe pair moved from `(0.75, 1.0)` to `(0.5, 1.0)` — a
   response to the *gate's* output, on the principle that the near probe belongs at
   the fitted region's edge, not at an arbitrary interior point. A numerical ceiling
   was added to the integrator after overflow warnings during the free-form fit; it
   is six orders of magnitude above anything any contestant reaches and exists so a
   divergent optimiser probe yields a finite residual. And the gate itself was
   rewritten — see point 4. Because of the ceiling, the sweep will not reproduce the
   table above exactly.
3. **The direction cuts against the experimenter.** The reading is *negative for
   declared constitutive forms*: the free-form baseline beats the correct declared
   form, and a tabular baseline beats both. Pre-registration exists to stop an
   experimenter tuning a threshold until their preferred result clears it. A peek
   that reveals a result contrary to the extension this repository is arguing for is
   not that failure mode. It remains a weakness, and it is why this section exists.
4. **The gate's first formulation was replaced, and that is a finding rather than a
   convenience.** Implemented as E-39 literally proposes — absolute disagreement
   against the declared minimum effect — the check returned **VACUOUS on M11.5's
   axis** (0.3529 against 0.6564). Rather than lower the bar, the two axes were
   measured side by side, which showed the criterion cannot separate them at any
   threshold and that divergence can. The replacement is a different *measurement*,
   not a relaxed one, and the axis it was replaced to admit is admitted by a factor
   of seven above the declared bar while the axis it must refuse is refused on all
   three criteria.

## 6. Standing limitations, stated in advance

**Standard errors are measured in-envelope**, because measuring them on the held-out
region would mean looking at the numbers the thresholds exist to judge. In-envelope
variability may understate held-out variability, which makes τ **tighter** and a
positive finding **easier** — anti-conservative in the direction of the claim.

**The gate is a dry run at shorter reach than the real hold-out**: it pushes to
`γ = 1.0` where the sweep pushes to `γ = 6.0`. It is therefore a conservative
proxy — a gate that passes at short reach may pass for the wrong reason at long
reach, though the failure it is designed to catch (a declared form with no
dependence on the axis) is reach-independent.

**A single generator.** ADR-045's Generator B is not part of M11.5 and no
cross-generator comparison will be computed.

## 7. Reproduction

```
python scripts/run_m11_5_preregistration.py
pytest tests/test_holdout_discrimination.py
```

Seeded throughout — `default_rng(1000+i)` for training draws, `default_rng(5000+i)`
for the independent in-envelope evaluation draws, `default_rng(3000)` for the gate's
probe, `default_rng(99)` for the withheld-term magnitude — so every value above is
reproducible at this commit.
